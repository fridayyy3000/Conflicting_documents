# Monarchy of the United Kingdom

Reference summary: title: Monarchy of the United Kingdom context: King Edward IV led the monarch to break from the Roman Catholic Church and to establish the Church of England (the Anglican Church) Wales – which had been conquered centuries earlier, but had remained a separate dominion – was annexed to England under the Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542 Edward IV's son and successor, the young Edward VI, continued with further religious reforms, but his early death in 1553 precipitated a succession crisis He was wary of allowing his Catholic elder half-sister Mary I to succeed, and therefore drew up a will designating Lady Jane Grey as his heiress Jane's reign was short-lived, as the country faced ongoing turmoil due to the Wars of the Roses and the struggle for power between the House of Lancaster and the House of York Edward IV's charisma, military prowess, and strong leadership played a significant role in these events
